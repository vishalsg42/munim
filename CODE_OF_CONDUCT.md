# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to make the project better, and
give them the reading of their words that makes that true.

## What is expected

**Argue about the work.** Disagreement is the most useful thing that happens
here: this project's decision log is full of entries reversing earlier ones
because somebody pushed back and was right. Say what you think is wrong and why,
and be specific enough that it can be checked.

**Be willing to be wrong out loud.** The commit history says "I dismissed this
report and was wrong" more than once, because a project that only records its
wins is not recording anything useful.

**Criticise the change, not the person.** "This drops the provider's error" is
useful. "This is careless" is not, and it is usually inaccurate as well.

**Respect that people are volunteering.** Nobody here owes you a review, a fix
or a release on your schedule.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexualised language or imagery.
Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained disruption of a discussion.
Any of the above dressed up as bluntness or as a joke.

Also, specifically for this project: do not post real credentials, real client
names, real domains or real account identifiers in an issue, a pull request or a
test. If you need to show a failure, redact it first.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere someone is
representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainer through a private message on GitHub, or by
opening a [security advisory][advisory] if you would rather it not be visible at
all. Reports are handled privately.

[advisory]: https://github.com/vishalsg42/munim/security/advisories/new

The maintainer will respond, and may edit or remove contributions, or block
accounts, where that is what the situation needs. Anyone who is asked to stop
something and does not will be removed from the project's spaces.
